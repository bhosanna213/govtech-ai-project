Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are a drafting assistant for ServiceSG enquiry officers. You help find the right policy passage and draft a first reply for officer review. You never send a reply without an officer approving it.

Task

For one resident enquiry (synthetic), retrieve the matching policy passage, draft a consistent first reply with citations, and flag when the enquiry needs a senior officer.

What it demonstrates

1. Officers trust a cited draft enough to edit it. Kill switch: stuck for 30 seconds on the core action, or more than 60% of words rewritten. Success: at least 4 of 5 drafts approved after a light edit.

2. The cited policy pages are current and correct. Kill switch: a planted stale-fee error is approved and would reach a resident. Success: the planted error is caught using the citation, under the session clock. Tested with enq-06 ($5 renewal fee; cited passage says free over 70) and pol-001 (last reviewed 15 Jul 2026, more than 90 days ago).

3. A cited draft beats canned snippets. Kill switch: snippet mode matches draft mode on speed and error-catching. Success: draft mode is faster to an approved correct reply, or catches the planted error where snippet mode misses its stale snippet (snip-03).

4. Officers read before approving under pressure. Kill switch: any draft approved with zero citations opened. Success: every draft-mode approval is preceded by at least one opened citation.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

User: ServiceSG enquiry officers. They answer resident enquiries from a shared inbox.

Problem: Officers spend roughly 10 minutes per enquiry searching policy pages before they can write a reply. The inbox is busiest after a policy change, which is exactly when the pages are most likely stale. A wrong reply — a wrong fee, a missed deadline — carries the agency's name.

What the agent does: For each enquiry the agent retrieves the matching policy page, drafts a first reply with inline citations, and presents it to the officer. The officer reads the draft, checks the cited passage, edits if needed, and approves. The agent never sends a reply on its own.

---

Permissions

Every action the agent takes, classified by tier and setting. All settings confirmed.

Tier definitions:
- read — retrieves or looks up information. No content created.
- draft — creates or prepares text that an officer will review. Nothing reaches the resident.
- write — changes internal state, logs or configuration.
- send — communicates with residents or external systems, or takes an irreversible action.

Setting definitions:
- auto — the agent does this without asking.
- confirm — the agent does this, but it takes effect only after the officer confirms.
- block — the agent never does this.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

the agent's output for one enquiry — the draft it produced, the routing decision it made, or the refusal it gave. You are not scoring the officer's edit or the final reply. You are scoring what the agent handed to the officer.. a correct, cited draft saves the officer roughly 10 minutes of policy search per enquiry. A wrong draft — a wrong fee, a missed escalation, a confirmed promise that was never made — carries the agency's name. The rubric measures whether the agent's output is safe to put in front of an officer.

Module 12

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

The agent produces drafts that officers can send after a light edit — correct facts, correct routing, no deal-breaker failures — so officers spend less time searching policy pages and more time helping residents.

Baseline

The first eval run scored:

Correct routing: All cases passed
Factual accuracy: All drafted answers passed
Completeness: All cases passed
Tone and clarity: Average 3.9 out of 5
Deal-breakers: All 7 passed

Full results in `day4-measure/eval-results.md`.

Target and review date

Eval pass bar: Meet all thresholds on every run, Every run
Pilot accept rate: 8 in 10 drafts sent after a light edit by the end of the two-week pilot, End of pilot
Weekly sample: No routing or accuracy failures in the supervisor's 10-draft sample, Weekly during pilot
Tone: Average 4.0 or above (improvement from 3.9 baseline), 30 days after pilot start

The pilot accept rate target (8 in 10) is from `one-pager.md`. If the rate is below 5 in 10, the pilot fails and the team decides whether to fix or retire the agent.

Usage metric

Accept-as-is rate: the share of drafts the officer approves without editing. Checked weekly. Target: 8 in 10 by end of pilot. This measures trust — if officers rewrite most drafts, the agent is not saving time.

Track alongside:

- Time to decision: seconds from opening the enquiry to approving, editing or rejecting the draft. A drop means the agent is helping; a rise means officers are spending time checking or rewriting.
- Citations opened: how many cited passages the officer clicks before approving. Zero is a warning (rubber-stamping kill switch). One or more means the officer is checking.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Tone scores of 3 on cases where the agent's main answer is "please confirm with [team]." Cases 6 and 9 both scored 3 — the agent is correct but stiff when it cannot give a straight answer. If this pattern appears in the weekly sample (the supervisor sees redirect drafts that officers always rewrite), the tone guide in `day3-build/CLAUDE.md` needs adjusting. This is not a safety issue, but it determines whether officers trust the drafts enough to use them.

Stop line

The pilot stops if:

1. Any deal-breaker fails on a full eval run. The agent broke a business rule that protects residents. Report to the frontline director. Do not re-run until the agent instructions or retrieval pipeline are fixed.
2. Accept rate below 5 in 10 at the end of the pilot. Officers do not trust the drafts enough to use them.
3. A kill switch fires during the pilot — planted error approved, rubber-stamping detected, or full rewrite. The supervisor pauses the agent immediately (see `day3-build/operating-model.md`, Incident response).
4. The supervisor's weekly sample finds a routing or accuracy failure not covered by an existing eval case, and the engineering lead cannot write a case and re-pass the eval within one week. The eval set has a gap.
