# Opportunity: Prioritising cross-institution STEP 2.0 enhancement requests during product discovery, from submission of an identified problem to a documented decision on whether to prioritise, defer or investigate further.

**Scenario:** C · MOE lower secondary biology

All quotes and metrics are synthetic and labelled as made up.

## Problem Framework

### 1. Goal

Enable STEP 2.0 product leaders and the six POLITE institutions to make timely, transparent and evidence-based prioritisation decisions, so that the most valuable learner, employer and CET operational problems receive attention without unnecessary coordination delays.

### 2. Problem

STEP's historical governance requires coordination across six institutions with different operational needs. We have yet to validate whether the proposed STEP 2.0 model provides sufficiently clear ownership, common prioritisation criteria and decision authority to resolve competing requests efficiently. Without these, the Product Office risks spending excessive time securing alignment rather than making customer-value-driven decisions.

### 3. Context

The setting is STEP 2.0 discovery and planning across five polytechnics and ITE. Primary participants include CET business owners, institutional representatives, the Product Office, technology leaders and governance bodies. Beneficiaries include adult learners, employers and CET officers. Requests may involve shared journeys, institutional workflows, funding rules or integrations. The revised programme targets 2031, making early product choices particularly important.

### 4. Constraints

The approach must respect mandatory institutional and government policies, security requirements, financial controls and formally assigned approval authorities. It must accommodate legitimate differences between IHLs and work within approved staffing, procurement and programme arrangements. The experiment cannot assume that historical governance has already changed or that the proposed Product Director has delegated authority that has not been formally established.

### 5. Success

In a pilot using five representative cross-IHL requests, at least four receive a documented prioritisation decision within ten working days. All five have a named decision owner, an explicit rationale referencing user or business evidence, and recorded approval or escalation requirements. No decision bypasses mandatory risk, policy or financial controls.

### 6. Explore

Can a shared prioritisation workflow, with common evaluation criteria, named decision rights and transparent institutional trade-offs, enable the Product Office to make timely decisions without weakening institutional trust or compliance? Or are unclear authority and governance arrangements the more fundamental constraints?

## Hypothesis

A lightweight, evidence-based prioritisation workflow with explicit decision rights can help the STEP 2.0 Product Office and six IHLs resolve competing enhancement requests faster, while preserving institutional accountability and necessary governance.

- Risk type: Value (build a landing page first)
- Kill switch: We assume the Product Office can obtain sufficient delegated authority and institutional agreement to conclude routine prioritisation decisions without repeated executive escalation. If it cannot, a faster workflow or decision dashboard alone will not solve the underlying problem.
- Kill-switch signal: Three or more of five representative requests remain undecided because decision authority is unclear or repeated executive escalation is necessary, despite agreed prioritisation criteria and complete supporting information.
- Success signal: By 28 February 2027, at least four out of five pilot requests receive a documented prioritisation decision within ten working days, with all five recording a named owner, evidence-based rationale and appropriate governance checks.
